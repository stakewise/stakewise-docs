# What is StakeWise?

<Image img={require('./img/what_is_stakewise.png')} alt="StakeWise staking methods: Vault Staking, Simple Staking (one-click), and running your own Vault" />

Operating across Ethereum and Gnosis Chain, StakeWise offers three ways to stake, each suited to a different type of staker:

[Vault Staking](/staker/vault-staking): For stakers who want control, choose a specific Vault from a [marketplace of Vaults ↗](https://app.stakewise.io/vaults) with its own fees, MEV strategy, and performance. Most Vaults have osToken minting available.

[Simple Staking](/staker/simple-staking): For stakers who want to start earning right away without picking a Vault, deposit ETH/GNO through the app in a few clicks. osToken is issued automatically.

[Run Own Vault](/operator/introduction): For individuals and organizations that want full control over their Vault setup to stake for themselves or offer staking to others and earn fees.

## Architecture
Behind these staking options, StakeWise is made up of three parts that work together: Vaults, osToken, and Oracles.

### Vaults: Where Staking Happens
A Vault is a staking pool. Stakers deposit ETH or GNO into it, and the Vault stakes those funds and collects the rewards. Anyone can open a Vault and set its own rules, such as fees and how it is run. Most Vaults accept deposits from anyone, while [private and blocklist Vaults](../vaults/vault-types) restrict who can stake. Each Vault stands on its own, so poor performance or a penalty in one Vault has no effect on stakers in the others.

:::custom-notes[Deep Dive]
Learn more about [Vaults](../vaults/intro)
:::

### osToken: Liquidity While Staking
Staked funds are normally locked and can't be used for anything else. osToken removes that limit. While staking in a Vault, stakers can mint osToken against their position: a token that keeps earning staking rewards but can still be traded or used across DeFi. It is overcollateralized — more ETH or GNO is always staked than the osToken issued against it — and comes as osETH on Ethereum and osGNO on Gnosis Chain.

Learn more about [osToken](../ostoken/intro)
:::

### Oracles: Keeping Everything in Sync
StakeWise's smart contracts run on Ethereum, but they need up-to-date information about what is happening on the <Tooltip content={<><strong>Beacon Chain</strong> <br /> Ethereum's consensus layer, a peer-to-peer network of consensus clients that handles block gossip and consensus logic. Oracles are a decentralized network that provides this information. They track validator activity, update rewards, and keep osToken's value accurate, without any single party being in control.
